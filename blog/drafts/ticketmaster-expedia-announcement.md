A while back I wrote about the bench of booking partners behind our guides, and I ended it with a line about closing the gap between "that sounds amazing" and "it's booked."

Two names just closed most of what was left. **Ticketmaster** and **Expedia** are now built into CityBucketList.com.

All three of us have something to say about it, so we are splitting this one three ways.

## Ticketmaster: the shows are real, and they are near you

![](/attractions/ticketmaster-logo.png "logo")

Open the Attractions page in any city and you no longer get a static list of landmarks. You get what is actually happening around you this week, pulled live. Denver, Nashville, Austin, Chicago, Pittsburgh: the page fills with that city's week.

![A crowd with their hands up at a night concert](/blog/ticketmaster-expedia/show.jpg "Photo: Nainoa Shizuru / StockSnap, CC0")

These come straight from [Ticketmaster](https://ticketmaster.evyy.net/c/7504721/264167/4272?u=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ticketmaster.com&subId1=blog_partners_ticketmaster), the official box office. Not resale, not a marketplace guessing at inventory. Verified tickets from the venue, with the seat map and the real price. That is the whole United States plus around two dozen countries.

The part I care about as a designer is what it did to the page. A venue card used to be a photograph and a paragraph. Now, if that venue has something on sale, the card tells you and hands you the ticket. Arenas and ballparks, and the small rooms too. In New York the page runs more than forty venues deep. In Pittsburgh, Club Cafe and the Funhouse at Mr. Smalls sit in the same grid as PPG Paints Arena. Every city gets its own version, which is the whole idea.

## Expedia: somewhere to sleep after the show

![](/travels/expedia-logo.svg "logo")

The other half of a trip is the bed. Our Travels page has been sitting there with a booking layout finished and a "Coming Soon" label on every button, waiting for a partner worth switching it on for.

![A hotel room made up and waiting](/blog/ticketmaster-expedia/stay.jpg "Photo: Travel Adventures / StockSnap, CC0")

[Expedia](https://prf.hn/click/camref:1110lLrVp/pubref:blog_partners_expedia/destination:https%3A%2F%2Fwww.expedia.com%2FHotel-Search) is that partner. Hotels, resorts, inns, and Vrbo rentals, all searchable and bookable from the page, with real availability and real prices.

So a weekend stops being four separate errands. Read the guide, get the tickets, get the room. Same site, same afternoon.

## What it means from the driver's seat
### Brian Uhler

I have been driving this valley a long time, and the calls that matter are the ones people plan. A concert downtown. A flight out of Pittsburgh International at six in the morning. A game where nobody wants to argue about parking.

What is different now is that the plan and the ride live in the same place. Someone finds the show, gets the ticket, and the venue address is right there when they go to schedule a car. They are not retyping an address into a second app at eleven at night.

One thing I would ask, and I speak for every independent driver on this platform: give us the notice. A scheduled ride works because somebody put it on their calendar. Twenty four hours is the courtesy. We are not an on-demand service and we have never pretended to be, and honestly that is the point. You know who is picking you up, and we know we can be there.

## What it means under the hood
### Justin Barber

The interesting problem was never the links. It was making a venue card know what is on sale at that venue tonight.

Events and places come from two different systems that do not agree on names. One says "PPG Paints Arena," the other might say "Mr Smalls Theatre" where we have it as "Theater." We had to teach the platform to match those up, then reach far enough into the events feed to find them. In a city like New York a handful of long-running shows can swallow an entire page of results before you ever get to a second venue.

Fix that and a card stops being a picture. It knows there is a Penguins game on Thursday and it can sell you the seat.

The ride hand-off got the same treatment. When you schedule a car to a venue, the destination is already filled in when you land in the app. Small thing. It is the difference between a booking and a chore.

## The whole point

City Bucket List has always been a software platform, not a travel agency and not a car company. We connect members with independent drivers and with the places worth going. What changed this month is how much of the trip you can finish without leaving.

Find the thing. Get the ticket. Book the room. Schedule the ride with a driver you actually know the name of.
